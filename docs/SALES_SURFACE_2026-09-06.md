# PressTalk: the sales surface and the first $500

Decision and execution packet, 6 September 2026. This supersedes the positioning, acquisition sequencing and research instrumentation proposed in `FUNNEL_DESIGN_2026-09-06.md`. It does not change existing customer rights. The source of the prepared public copy is `site/index.html`, with a German entry at `site/de/index.html`. Ad and outreach copy is in `docs/launch/ADS.md` and `docs/launch/POSTS.md`.

**Sell the sentence, offer a real trial, and buy a small answer before buying reach.** Start with German-speaking Apple Silicon Mac owners who write replies every day and already wish they could say them instead. German is the first acquisition market because the developer can demonstrate and support that workflow. The benchmark is supporting evidence, not the positioning.

## 1. What was wrong, and the actual page

The old surface asked the wrong visitor to do the wrong work. It invited a technical reader to inspect a recognizer rather than showing someone their next reply appearing. “Your Mac already hears everything” introduced anxiety; “I publish my error rate” made the developer the protagonist. The two measurement sections demanded a comparison before the visitor had a reason to care. The illustration required a press, explained itself at paragraph length, and typed letters after its claimed measured delay even though delivery is a single insertion. The only commercial action was an email near the bottom. A stranger could neither experience the app nor buy without waiting for its author.

Changing adjectives would leave that structure intact. Change the order and the action.

## 2. Positioning in the buyer’s words

Five minutes earlier: **“I know what I want to say. I just don’t want to type this whole reply.”**

The selected headline is **“Think it. Say it. There it is.”** The subhead immediately supplies Mac, Fn and the destination. The headline describes the desired result; the visible action explains how it happens. For search ads, where there is no room for a film, be more literal: **“Sprich statt zu tippen.”**

Three rejected headlines:

| Headline | Why it loses |
|---|---|
| “The Mac dictation app that publishes its error rate.” | Explains the author’s diligence. The buyer still has to infer a benefit. |
| “Better German dictation than Apple.” | Starts an argument; the lab comparison does not establish a better complete experience for this person. |
| “Perfect words. Every time.” | Describes a desirable result with an indefensible guarantee. A single wrong proper noun breaks the promise. |

This does not require the buyer to care about speech-recognition technology or already resent a competitor. The first paid audience is German-speaking Mac owners actively looking for dictation. Expansion follows purchases and support capacity, not the size of a generic productivity audience.

## 3. The machine that can sell unattended

There are two distinct jobs: handle a stranger who arrives, and cause a stranger to arrive. A beautiful static site does only the first.

| 14-day local trial | Let the buyer hear their own voice and use their own app before paying | Policy exists; enforcement is advisory today. Finish and test it before advertising a commercial trial. No analytics, activation callbacks or usage counters. |

Automatic fulfilment means a verified successful order triggers issuance of the existing signed offline licence and receipt delivery. Validate the order event, deduplicate retries, keep the signing secret out of the website, and make a lost receipt recoverable without making the app call a server. Prefer a small managed transaction handler; there is no need for a cron job, marketing automation system or new app service. Check what Lemon Squeezy can deliver natively before building the bridge: a provider-generated activation key is not automatically compatible with this app’s offline signed-licence format. Test success, duplicate event, delayed event and refund handling before selling unattended. Do not invent remote revocation of an offline licence; honour the refund and prevent duplicate fulfilment or reissue.

Receipt copy:

> **Your PressTalk licence**
>
> Thanks for buying PressTalk. Open PressTalk → Settings → Enter Licence Key… Paste the key below and choose Activate. Keep a copy of this email. Your licence is checked on your Mac; you do not need an account or an activation connection.
>
> Every future Mac update we release is included. For setup help or a refund within 14 days, reply to this email.

Include the actual signed `PRESSTALK-1.…` key in the receipt. This matches the existing paste-and-activate UI; verify the delivered key in the downloaded build before enabling automatic fulfilment.

Do not gate the download behind a mailing list. The usable trial is the lead-generation asset. No abandoned-cart sequence, retargeting audience, forced newsletter consent or daily content schedule. A buyer’s receipt is transactional; future promotional mail would need its own explicit opt-in and is outside this initial machine.

**Time budget:** allow 12–20 hours of one-time implementation and release work for trial, first-run fixes, fulfilment and fresh-install checks; this is an estimate, not completed work. If it exceeds 20 hours, reduce launch scope and defer paid acquisition instead of creating manual work every day. Owner launch/research work is another 5–6 hours in week one. After launch: 30 minutes acquisition/order review, 45 minutes support/refunds, 30 minutes one distribution batch, and 15 minutes reserve: **2 hours/week**. Pause the next campaign if support needs more than its allowance; continue serving existing buyers. Maintenance and macOS release work are occasional additional product costs, not hidden inside “passive.”

## 4. The first marketing dollars

**Maximum learning budget: $500, not a recurring monthly commitment.** Signing/membership and product development are separate sunk/setup costs, not advertising spend. The cap is USD equivalent; if the ad account bills EUR, convert each tranche when funding it and record actual invoice costs in one currency. Do not assume an exchange rate.

Why $500: five observations can expose a bad first use, roughly $100 can expose an unaffordable search auction, and two repetitions can distinguish one encouraging result from a channel worth continuing. More money before those answers mainly purchases a larger version of the same uncertainty. The defaults are spending caps and bid ceilings, not forecasts of traffic or sales.

### Economics first

Choose a $20 advertised total including applicable tax. Lemon Squeezy supports tax-inclusive pricing; its setting is store-wide, so inspect existing store products before changing it. [Tax-inclusive pricing](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/sales-tax-vat).

Illustrative German card purchase, assuming 19% VAT, standard fees, international surcharge and non-US bank payout:

`($20 / 1.19 - ($20 × 6.5% + $0.50)) × 99% = $14.86`

At $39 under those assumptions, approximately $29.44. These are calculated proceeds before refunds, support, product maintenance and income tax; PayPal and settlement details can reduce them. Verify against the store’s actual orders. The documented base is 5% + $0.50, with applicable international and payout fees. [Lemon Squeezy fees](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/fees).

Founder acquisition target: **$8 per non-refunded, source-supported purchase**. **$15 is a stop line**, already beyond the illustrative contribution before labour. A $0.50 click requires 6.25% click-to-purchase conversion to hit $8; a $1 click requires 12.5%. At 2% purchase conversion, the maximum affordable click is $0.16. Those are hurdles, not predicted conversion rates. At $39, reconsider a $15 target only after measuring actual purchase behaviour at that price; do not pretend the higher price preserves conversion.

**Broad cold paid acquisition is not a credible base-case business model for the $20 offer.** Narrow search might clear these hurdles. That is why the experiment is small. There is no recurring revenue to justify an invented lifetime value, and no reason to spend future $39 revenue acquiring today’s $20 buyer.

### Measurement without instrumenting the app

Maintain one manual ledger: `campaign | dates | spend | ad clicks | aggregate download requests | source-supported orders | unknown-source orders | refunds | net receipts | volunteered problems`. No app event, install ping, account, device ID, local usage counter, “privacy-friendly” activation summary or researcher beacon. Trial entitlement state is functional state, not an analytics feed.

Ad-platform spend/clicks and Lemon Squeezy orders/refunds are sufficient for the economic test. Download-request totals, if the host already makes them available in aggregate, are optional debugging context. Do not retain raw IPs or create fingerprinted “unique visitors” for marketing. Download requests are not installs, first dictations or active users. Do not sum Homebrew and GitHub download totals as different people.

For visitors who go directly to checkout, carry only a coarse allowlisted label such as `google_de_1` from the current page URL into `checkout[custom][campaign]`. No cookie, localStorage, per-person token or app handoff. Disclose the checkout source label. Lemon returns checkout custom data with the transaction. A person who tries the app and later buys from its generic link has **unknown source** unless they volunteer an answer. Optional receipt question: **“Where did you find PressTalk? A one-word reply is plenty. You can ignore this.”** No nonbuyer email capture. [Lemon Squeezy checkout custom data](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/checkout/passing-custom-data).

Run only one paid channel at a time. For spend `S`, confirmed source-supported retained purchases `A`, and otherwise-unassigned retained purchases `U`, inspect:

- Conservative observed CAC: `S/A`. If `A=0`, no finite confirmed CAC.
- Most generous possible CAC: `S/(A+U)`. Unknown orders cannot be counted in two campaigns or include testers/old grandfathered users.

Continue only when conservative CAC meets the target. Stop when even the generous bound crosses the stop line. If the two bounds straddle it, the economic answer is unknown: pause and use optional buyer replies, not more tracking. Low attribution can prevent scaling; it does not justify violating the product promise.

**Calendar honesty:** a seven-day ad flight and a 14-day trial cannot establish mature paid conversion in a week. Week one can reject comprehension, setup and affordable reach. Stop spending at the seven-day cap. Give the last click 21 days for trial and decision, then reconcile refunds 14 days after each counted purchase. Results are provisional until that refund window closes; late purchases update the ledger. Do not pay for “more data” while waiting. Second-channel tests wait until the first decision window ends so unknown sales have a meaningful bound.

## 6. The piece most likely to be wrong

**The buyer may enjoy the demonstration and still prefer typing tomorrow.** That is the weakest assumption, more fundamental than which headline wins. A perfect transcript of a canned sentence does not prove that speaking fits a workday, an open office, or the buyer’s normal replies.

Within one week, recruit the five strangers above. Give each the signed app and ask them to use three real, non-sensitive replies they were already going to write. Observe setup without coaching; let them compare typing or their existing dictation on comparable replies, including any correction time. Pay for their time regardless of their opinion. Ask separately for permission for one follow-up on day 3 or 5: **“Did you choose PressTalk for another real reply? If not, what did you use instead?”** No in-app usage report, no silent follow-up tracking, no testimonial request disguised as research.

The operating gate is 4/5 reach useful text unaided and 3/5 independently report choosing it again on a different day. Five people are not market validation, and compensated purchases are excluded. This can expose a broken promise or a habit that never forms. Failure to recruit five within the capped attempt is an acquisition problem, not evidence against usefulness.

If installation blocks them, fix that single step and repeat with fresh people; do not rewrite the hero. If they can use it but have no reason to return, narrow the entire next experiment to **German email replies for people already using dictation at least three days a week**, with the headline **“Sag deine nächste E-Mail.”** Demonstrate their own names and corrections.
