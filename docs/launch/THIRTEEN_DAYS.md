# Thirteen days

Astra's plan for the burst that tests one question: **can the ownership story
recruit strangers who actually use and buy this?**

Not a launch. A test with a stop threshold, written down before it starts so it
cannot be argued with afterwards.

## What is being tested, and what is not

Every expensive thing is already paid for. The app is notarized, the site is
live at presstalk.app, checkout works, the trial enforces. None of that is
evidence about anything, because no stranger has touched it.

The owner uses PressTalk daily and cancelled a paid subscription for it. That
is real, and it is evidence about **one person who built the vocabulary, knows
the failure modes and can repair it himself**. Strangers bring different
speech, different hardware and less patience.

## Target

| | |
|---|---|
| Plan | **100 qualified leads** |
| Stretch | 500 |
| Purchases by day 13 | 5–10 |
| Read the cohort again | day 20 |

The funnel behind it: 50,000 relevant views × 1% reaching the site × 20%
becoming contactable = 100 leads. Fifty thousand views is thirty uploads
averaging a thousand, plus one placement carrying twenty thousand. Neither is
guaranteed. Weak content and no placements produce fewer than twenty-five.

**On a million leads in thirteen days.** Astra searched for a comparable case
and found none: no €20 direct-download Mac utility has done it from zero
audience. It found the closest counterexample and reported it fairly — Locket
did two million signups in eleven days on TikTok, from about twenty testers,
though later reporting disclosed a $2.5M round around launch. A million remains
a possible exceptional event. It cannot be the operating forecast.

And the arithmetic correction that matters more: **a million leads is not a €20
million business.** At 5% conversion that is 50,000 customers and €1M gross. A
million *buyers* needs twenty million leads.

## Stop threshold

**Fewer than three unrelated people who each install unaided, pay in full
without refunding, and report using it on three separate days — by day 13.**
Or more than 33 hours spent.

Do not extend the deadline because two people were enthusiastic.

Failing to recruit enough people establishes that *this* acquisition attempt
cannot work within these constraints. It does not prove nobody wants the app.
That distinction still permits a firm decision to stop.

## What to record: 30 minutes

| Segment | Minutes | Produces |
|---|---:|---|
| Main script, two takes and repairs | 8 | Founder film, ownership excerpts |
| Screen demonstrations | 6 | English reply, German reply, offline |
| Six banter prompts | 12 | Six answers up to two minutes |
| Short hooks and German pickup | 4 | Platform openings, native German asset |

**Skip the digital avatar.** This launch depends on seeing the actual person who
built and uses the app. An avatar adds production work and weakens the only
evidence that cannot be faked. Captions carry accessibility and language.

### The script

Read conversationally. Bracketed directions are not spoken. About three minutes
including demonstrations.

> I bought the Mac. Why am I renting the dictation?
>
> I was paying for Wispr Flow. I used it. Then I built a small app for the way I
> actually work, and I cancelled my subscription.
>
> It's called PressTalk. Here's what it does.
>
> *[Show an actual blank email draft. Hold Fn.]*
>
> "Hi Sam, Thursday at ten works for me. I'll send you the notes beforehand."
>
> *[Release Fn. Show the real wait and the resulting text.]*
>
> Hold the key. Say the sentence. Let go.
>
> I use this for replies, notes, and the thoughts I already know how to say. The
> words go into the app I'm using.
>
> PressTalk costs twenty euros once.
>
> There's no subscription. You don't need an account. After the speech model
> downloads during setup, recognition runs on your Mac.
>
> *[Show a local note with Wi-Fi disconnected, on a Mac with no other active
> internet connection.]*
>
> "This sentence is being transcribed without an internet connection."
>
> *[Show the result at its actual speed.]*
>
> That's also part of how a small app can cost twenty euros. Your Mac does the
> speech recognition.
>
> When I say you keep it, I mean your licence is checked on your Mac. It doesn't
> need an activation server to keep giving you permission.
>
> Every future Mac release I actually publish is included. I can't promise what
> every future version of macOS will do. You can keep using the version you have
> on compatible Macs.
>
> Apple already includes free dictation. Try that too. Use the same three
> sentences, include the time you spend correcting them, and keep whichever
> works better for you.
>
> I built PressTalk because I wanted this particular tool in my day. I use it
> every day.
>
> You can try it for three days, with no card and no account. You'll need an
> Apple Silicon Mac running macOS fourteen or later.
>
> Go to presstalk dot app.
>
> Try the next reply you were going to type. If PressTalk earns a place on your
> Mac, it's twenty euros once.

**Do not replace a transcript or speed up the recognition.** Keep any correction
needed to understand the actual result. The whole asset is worthless if a viewer
later finds it was staged.

### Banter prompts

| Prompt | What we hope to catch |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about the moment you cancelled Wispr Flow. What did you like, and what had PressTalk actually replaced?" | A credible decision story with specifics |
| "Why twenty euros? How can you afford to sell it once?" | A natural explanation of local computation and scope |
| "What tool have you owned for years and still enjoy using?" | Warmth and a tangible ownership analogy |
| "Apple Dictation is free. When should someone keep using that?" | Confidence and an honest buying criterion |
| *"Zeig mir einen Satz aus deinem echten Alltag. Was musst du danach noch korrigieren?"* | Natural speech and useful proof |
| *"Wie würdest du einem Freund erklären, warum du kein weiteres Abo wolltest?"* | The founder's own memorable phrasing |

## Twelve assets, thirty uploads

One 3-minute founder film. One 30–40 second uninterrupted demonstration. Ten
20–45 second shorts: ownership, the cancellation story, why €20, the key
demonstration, offline, licence ownership, Apple's free alternative, and three
German pieces.

**Thirty uploads, not thirty ideas.** The same ten shorts across Shorts, Reels
and TikTok. Budget five hours for editing including learning it. Shotcut is a
free Mac editor with subtitle tools.

### Hooks, first three seconds

| Platform | Hook |
|---|---|
| TikTok | *"I cancelled a subscription and built this instead."* |
| Instagram Reels | *"One less subscription. Watch this."* |
| YouTube Shorts | *"Offline Mac dictation. Twenty euros."* |
| YouTube film | *"I cancelled Wispr Flow for this."* |
| X | *"Your Mac can do this offline."* |
| LinkedIn | *"I stopped renting my dictation app."* |
| German | *"Dein Mac. Deine Stimme. Kein Abo."* |

**The mobile-to-Mac gap is the hard part.** Shorts descriptions and comments
carry no clickable links. TikTok normally needs 1,000 followers or a business
account for a website link. Put **presstalk.app visibly on screen** and check
each account's link path before publishing.

## The pitch

Twenty-five of these. Small Mac creators with relevant recent videos,
specialist newsletter editors, community organisers. Large reviewers are
optional upside.

> I built PressTalk after cancelling my own Wispr Flow subscription. It's €20
> once, runs speech recognition locally, and has an offline licence. Your recent
> [specific item] made me think it might interest your audience. Here's a
> 35-second unedited demonstration and the three-day trial. If you test it,
> please show your own result — including anything it gets wrong.

Offer an independent test with no obligation to publish or to praise.

## Reading the result

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Attention, no visits | The content entertains people who do not want the product, or the path to a Mac is too hard |
| Visits, no trials | Compatibility, trust, or installation friction |
| Trials, no purchases | Everyday usefulness, correction effort, or the offer |
| Purchases, no recommendations | Possibly a viable utility business; organic expansion unproven |

A handful of conversions is not a rate. Use it to choose the next experiment,
not to forecast.

## What survives day thirteen either way

Reusable demonstrations, a clearer buying experience, and the first reviewer
relationships. And a tool the owner uses every day and no longer rents.

## The constraint on whoever helps

Astra's closing line, which is aimed at the assistant more than the owner:

> If those six hours turn into another roadmap, benchmark expansion or feature
> cycle, the argument for stopping wins.

The engineering is finished. Everything from here is talking to people.
