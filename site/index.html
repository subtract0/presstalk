What actually happens

  1. 01

    Hold

    Fn / Globe by default. Option, Option-Space, F5 or a trackpad hold if you prefer.

  2. 02

    Speak

    Recognition runs on the Neural Engine. Nothing is sent anywhere.

  3. 03

    Release

    Letting go is the endpoint, not a pause the app has to interpret. It waits a moment for you to finish the word.

  4. 04

    Paste

    Into the app you were already in. If it can't paste, it copies rather than losing your words.

Read these honestly. The audio is synthesised speech in three voices, not 144 different people in 144 different rooms, so the error rate is a regression baseline rather than a promise about your voice. The timings come from replayed audio on one machine. PressTalk has not been benchmarked against any other dictation app, so this page will not tell you it is quicker or more accurate than one.

Measured against the dictation you already have

The real alternative is not another paid app. It is the microphone key already on your Mac, which is free and now runs on-device too. So that is what we measured against, on the same 144 German clips, scored by the same function.

About a third fewer word errors on this set. Both sides get the same German vocabulary correction, and both numbers are the default configuration, so this is like for like. Three honest limits: the audio is synthesised speech in three voices rather than people in rooms; this compares recognition engines, not the full Dictation experience with its own punctuation and correction behaviour; and most of the brand and technical terms in that vocabulary list also appear in the test sentences, which flatters both scores. A reason to try PressTalk, not a finished proof.

Why local matters here

Cloud dictation works well and costs about $12–15 every month, forever, and every sentence you dictate goes through somebody's server. For a lot of work that is fine. For the sentence about your health, your salary, or the client you are about to lose, it is a decision you made once and now make every day without noticing.

PressTalk removes the decision. Recognition happens on the Neural Engine in your Mac. Turn off Wi-Fi after setup and dictation still works — that is the test, and you should run it.

What's the catch?

The awkward bits

Apple hasn't finished checking it yet. Notarization is paid for and in Apple's queue. Until it clears, macOS shows a warning on first launch and you approve it once in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. The instructions ship with the download. It is a queue, not a verdict.

Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later. No Intel build, no Windows, no iPhone. An M1 Air is plenty — the whole point is that it runs on hardware you already own.

I am one person. Support is me, usually within a day or two. There is no company behind this and no support contract. If that is not enough for your situation, don't buy it yet.
